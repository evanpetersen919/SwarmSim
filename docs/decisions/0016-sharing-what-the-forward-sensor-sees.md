# 0016. Sharing what the forward sensor sees, and what that is worth when the link is bad

Date: 2026-10-23. Status: proposed.

## Context

The project is about swarms whose communications are degraded. Everything built since ADR 0012 for the fixed-wing aircraft is about one aircraft's own sensing: each aircraft senses, avoids, and keeps its own counsel. The aircraft do talk, but only to say where they are (neighbor reports), so the one thing the swarm could share that matters to flight safety, what its aircraft have seen of the ground, is not shared. ADRs 0014 and 0015 named this as the decision after them, and it is the one that brings the project's question to the aircraft: what is shared sensing worth, and how much of that worth survives loss, latency, and jamming?

Why there is something to gain, from numbers of the scenario of ADR 0014 (six aircraft on a 300 m orbit at 31.3 m/s, computed from those values):

- The aircraft ahead on the circle reaches a hill about 10.0 s before the one behind (the spacing along the circle is 314 m), and a lap is 60.2 s.
- The forward sensor sees 25.6 s ahead in clear air (800 m) and only 6.4 s ahead in the fog of the experiments (200 m).
- A quarter turn at 0.9 of the bank limit has a radius of 159 m and takes 8.0 s, and a climb at the planning budget (1.8 m/s) gains 18 m in 10 s.

In fog, then, an aircraft sees a hill with less time than a quarter turn needs, while the aircraft ahead of it saw the same hill 10 s sooner. In ADR 0014 the fog cost the forward sensor and avoidance most (the forward sensor alone lost 3.40 and 4.50 of six aircraft on 400 and 480 m hills, avoidance as built 0.67 and 1.50), and in ADR 0015 with all three changes 0.60 and 1.57 were still lost in fog, against 0.37 and 0.57 in clear air. A warning from the aircraft ahead is the one remedy in reach for that.

There is also a thing that has to be separated from communication. Terrain does not move. An aircraft that saw a hill on one lap can know it on the next without being told. Any benefit of a shared store of what was seen is partly a benefit of memory, which needs no link, and a result that credits the link with it would be wrong. The experiment must include memory without communication as its own arm; the value of the link is the difference between sharing and memory, not between sharing and nothing.

What the code offers. Aircraft exchange beacons, lossy point-to-point messages sent to every other aircraft (`DroneAgent::step`); the channel loses, delays, and jams them like any other beacon (`channel_model.cpp`); only `MessageType::kCommand` is treated as reliable. The brain sends a neighbor report in `BrainOutput::report` and decodes whatever beacon arrives as a neighbor report. The wire format of the socket transport validates the message type (`net/src/wire.cpp`), so a new type needs that updated. The binary log records message events by outcome (sent, delivered, lost, rate limited, jammed), not by type, so a new type adds events and no log field.

Everything about what is shared and how it is used is a modeling choice, as in ADRs 0012 to 0015.

## Decision

1. **A hazard report is a new message type.** `MessageType::kHazard = 3`, unreliable and lossy like a beacon (the channel needs no change), with a payload in `control/` beside the command codec: little-endian, `u8 version | u64 send_tick | f32 sender position sigma | u8 count | count x (f32 x, f32 y, f32 z)`, at most 8 points, so at most 110 bytes. The coordinates are the sender's pose-estimate frame, like the neighbor reports'; positions are quantized to single precision, which is a few centimeters at a kilometer. The codec follows the neighbor report's rules: a count over the limit, a non-finite value, or a negative sigma is an error status and not an exception, and a malformed report is counted and ignored, as a malformed command or beacon is. The socket wire format accepts the new type.
2. **What is shared.** The returns of the sender's own forward sensor that matter at its flight level: those whose point is higher than the sender's altitude less its planning clearance. At most `points_per_message` of them (default 8), the highest first, one per 20 m cell. A report is sent every `period_ticks` (default the neighbor report period) while the sender has seen such a return within the last `repeat_s` (default 2 s), and carries the sender's current set, so a point that is still in view is sent again with every report. That repetition is the redundancy: a point sent n times over a link that loses a share p of messages is lost with probability p^n, and at 10 reports a second for 2 s that is 20 sends.
3. **What is kept.** Each aircraft keeps a bounded store of hazard points, one per 20 m cell, the highest point of the cell, with the tick it was last seen and its uncertainty (the range sigma of the sensor, added in quadrature to the sender's position sigma for a point that was received). A point expires after `ttl_s` (default 120 s, two laps). The store holds at most `max_points` (512) cells and drops the oldest first. It is a `std::map` keyed by cell, so its order, and so every result that depends on it, is a function of the data and not of the platform.
4. **How it is used.** Stored points join the avoidance assessment as extra points: a point is judged unclearable exactly as a return of the sensor is (the climb it needs, at the time to reach it, against what the aircraft can climb), it is given the column of its bearing when that is within half a column spacing of a column's azimuth and is ignored for the choice of column otherwise, and its uncertainty moves it in the safe direction as a range error does. They also join the altitude floor, with the same discount for the distance, but only for points whose bearing is within the horizontal field of the sensor, so the floor is not raised by ground that is behind or beside the aircraft. So a hill that the aircraft ahead saw acts on the aircraft behind before its own sensor sees it, and acts again on later laps.
5. **Modes.** `control.share.mode` is `off` (the default, byte-identical to ADR 0015), `memory` (the aircraft's own returns are stored and used, nothing is sent or received), or `share` (memory, and the reports are sent and received). Memory is part of `share`, so the difference between the two is the link. The keys `ttl_s`, `points_per_message`, `period_ticks`, `repeat_s` are written to the canonical text only when set. It needs `control.avoid`, which gives the points a use, and the scenario builder copies the sensor's horizontal field into it.
6. **Nothing changes in the log, the metrics, or the estimator rule.** The messages are events of the existing kinds, so `messages_sent` and `delivery_ratio` count them with the beacons; a hazard message cannot be told from a beacon in the log, which the analysis has to keep in mind. The store is in the brain's state hash only when the mode is not `off`, so earlier hashes are unchanged.
7. **Trust.** A received point is used if it decodes and is within the bounds of a scene, and not otherwise. A false or malicious report would make aircraft avoid ground that is not there, and is not defended against here (see not in scope).

## How it will be judged

The scenario and measures of ADR 0015, with the arm all three as the baseline, so that the new arms are compared with the best result so far, with the levels fixed and committed before the first run, 30 seeds per cell, paired by seed. A probe may size the jam and the loss levels but is not part of the results.

- **Arms.** Three: `avoid` (all three changes of ADR 0015, no memory); `memory` (the same with `share.mode: memory`); `share` (`share.mode: share`).
- **Communication conditions.** Loss 0.05 (the base), 0.30, and 0.60, independent; and no jam against one constant jammer over the whole area for 20 s to 60 s of the run at 0.97 loss on the receiver, as in the demos, so that the aircraft are cut off for most of a lap.
- **Environment.** Hills of 40 m (a control with nothing to share), 400 m, and 480 m; clear air and the fog of 375 m visibility; no map. 3 x 2 x 3 x 2 x 3 = 108 cells, 3,240 runs.
- **Criteria, fixed now.** (C1, the link is worth something) in fog on 400 and 480 m hills, at loss 0.05 and no jam, `share` against `memory`: late contacts lower with an interval entirely below zero, or the corridor share higher with an interval entirely above zero and late contacts not higher beyond the interval. (C2, the link does no harm) on the 40 m control, `share` against `memory` and against `avoid`: no late contacts, and a corridor share not lower by more than 0.02. (C3, it degrades gracefully) at every communication condition, `share` against `memory`: late contacts not higher beyond the paired interval (an interval entirely above zero is a harm). (C4, memory is separated from the link) the memory and share arms are both reported against `avoid`, so that a benefit that memory alone gives is credited to memory. A result that meets C2 and C3 and not C1 is the finding that the link adds nothing the sensor and memory do not give in this scenario; it is reported as such.
- **What is reported besides the tables.** The advantage of `share` over `memory` as a function of the loss, with intervals, and under the jam, which is the answer to the project's question for this scenario; the delivery ratio of the run (hazard messages and beacons together) for each communication condition.
- **Safety measures.** `late_contacts`, `ground_contacts`, `launch_contacts`, `first_ground_contact_s`, `min_agl_m`. **Cost measures.** `orbit_corridor_share`, `time_on_station`, `orbit_radial_rmse_m`, `mean_agl_m`, `collision_episodes`, `min_separation_m`, and `messages_sent`, which says what the sharing costs the channel.
- **A sensitivity sweep** of `ttl_s` (30, 120, 240 s) and `points_per_message` (2 and 8) for the `share` arm in fog on 400 m hills at loss 0.30: 6 cells, 180 runs.

## Alternatives considered

- **Share every return, or a patch of the sender's map.** A reading is 21 rays and a map patch is large; the selection of what matters at flight level carries the same information in 110 bytes. If the experiment shows that what was selected out mattered, that is the next change.
- **Share only while the sender is threatened.** It would lose the memory of a hill after the sender has passed it and cost the followers the lead; the repeat time is the middle course.
- **A reliable transport** (as for commands). It would make the result a result of an assumption the project is about relaxing; the lossy channel is the point.
- **Voting or confidence of reports before using them.** It is what a defense against false reports would need, and it needs a source of false reports to design against; it follows if the trust question is taken up.
- **Relays and multihop.** The swarm is one orbit and every aircraft is a neighbor of every other, so there is nothing to relay in this scenario.
- **A learned prior from the first lap.** It would be a map, and the decision about a map of the terrain from the aircraft's own flight is a different one.

## Not in scope

False or malicious reports (the store accepts what decodes, and an aircraft that sends bad points can cause needless avoidance; this is a limit), bandwidth (the channel limits messages per sender per tick, not bytes, and the experiment does not set the limit), navigation errors that grow in fog and are inherited by the points (the sender's sigma is carried, and what the points are worth with a drifting pose is a result, not something this decision corrects), hazards between aircraft (the separation rule keeps them), a quadrotor version, and any claim about the real aircraft.

## Consequences

- The brain gains a store and a message type, and the wire format a value; the tests must cover the codec (the layout byte for byte, the limits, a malformed report), the store (one point per cell, the highest kept, expiry, the bound and what is dropped first, the order), the use (a stored hill acts before the sensor sees it, a point outside the fan does not raise the floor, a point between columns does not choose a turn), the three modes (and that `off` leaves a run unchanged to the hash), and a transport test that the new type crosses a socket.
- Messages and events rise: each report is sent to every other aircraft, so five messages for each report of one aircraft, on top of the beacons; `messages_sent` and the pooled `delivery_ratio` change with the mode, and results with `share` are not comparable with runs without it on those columns.
- A memory that outlives its reason: a stored point is not removed when the terrain it describes is out of the way, and with a drifting pose a point can be placed wrongly; the ttl and the sensitivity sweep are there to show what the cost is.
- Everything about the rule is a modeling choice. The results say what such sharing is worth in this simulator, not what it is worth to the real aircraft.
