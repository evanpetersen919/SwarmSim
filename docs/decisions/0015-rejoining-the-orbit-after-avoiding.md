# 0015. Rejoining the guidance after avoiding, and judging a threat against what the vehicle can do

Date: 2026-10-23. Status: accepted.

## Context

ADR 0014 built avoidance by turning and slowing, and measured it (docs/RESULTS.md). It keeps most of the remaining aircraft off the ground where climbing cannot, and it costs the formation: on 400 m hills with no map the late ground contacts of six aircraft go from 2.03 to 0.30, while time on station goes from 0.30 to 0.07 and the radial error to the orbit from 12 to 202 m. With an exact map, where nothing was in danger, it still costs the formation (time on station 0.71 to 0.89 against 0.94 to 1.00 with the forward sensor alone). The outcome section of that ADR names two causes to look at, and this ADR looks at them.

What the flown path shows. A trace of three aircraft in one run (400 m hills, no map, seed 1, avoidance on; positions from `swarm_sim positions`) shows that the aircraft do come back to the orbit, but only after wide excursions. Their distance from the orbit center swings between about 300 m and 500 to 770 m and back, with a period of about 50 s: drone 1, for example, is at 303, 451, 432, 552, 589, 769, and 364 m at 0, 18, 27, 36, 48, 63, and 84 s. All of them also climb from 100 m to about 200 m, which is the altitude floor and not the turning. So the formation is not lost for good, as the results section guessed: it is torn open by a series of detours.

Why each detour is large, and why there are detours that are not needed, from the rule and the geometry:

- **The tie goes outward.** When the free columns on both sides are equally near the heading, the aircraft turns right. The orbit is counterclockwise, so the center is on the left and right is away from it. Every tie dodges outward, and the orbit then has to bring the aircraft back in.
- **The free column is chosen nearest the aircraft's own heading, not nearest where the guidance is taking it.** An aircraft on a circle that must dodge a hill ahead takes the smallest turn, which is not the turn that keeps it on the circle.
- **A return is judged against a climb the aircraft is not asked for.** Two things make the judgment stricter than the danger. The climb needed is to the ground plus the planning clearance (60 m), so the rule fires when the aircraft cannot keep 60 m, not when it cannot avoid the ground. And what the aircraft may count on is `climb_fraction` (0.6) of the climb limit times the time, a straight line that ignores the vehicle's vertical acceleration limit (2 m/s^2), which is the only thing that makes the ramp up to the climb rate a fact. The first is probably the larger cause of the false alarms with an exact map, where every trigger is one.
- **The cost measure cannot tell a small detour from a large one.** `time_on_station` counts the samples within 30 m of the aircraft's own point on its circle, so any detour, small or large, loses the phase and then the station for a long time. It cannot separate an excursion of 100 m from one of 500 m. A measure of how long the aircraft stays near its circle (cross-track only) can.

The aircraft's phase on its circle (the orbit reference is a function of time) is lost during any detour, and the aircraft then catches up with a speed adjustment that its speed limits bound; this ADR does not change that.

Everything here is a modeling choice, as in ADR 0014.

## Decision

1. **A rejoin preference for the choice of column, from the guidance itself.** The brain has the horizontal acceleration that the guidance asks for now (the orbit, the racetrack, the lanes, the formation: whatever produced `track`). With `control.avoid.rejoin` on, the preferred heading is the direction of the horizontal velocity after `turn_time_s` under that acceleration, `v + a_guidance * turn_time_s`, which is the way the guidance is already turning the aircraft, and it needs no knowledge of the pattern's geometry. The free column chosen is the one whose direction is nearest that heading, not the one nearest the aircraft's own; a tie goes to the side of the guidance acceleration's sideways component (toward the circle on a circle, whichever side the aircraft is on) and, when that is zero, to the right as before. The trigger is unchanged: the column nearest the aircraft's own heading is blocked. If the guidance acceleration is zero or the velocity after the turn time has no horizontal part, the rule of ADR 0014 applies.
2. **Whether a return can be cleared is a question of the vehicle's kinematics.** `control.avoid.feasibility` is `budget` (the default, the judgment of ADR 0014) or `kinematic`. In `kinematic`, the altitude the aircraft can gain in time `t` from its present vertical speed `v0` is, with `c` the vehicle's climb limit (the one the scenario builder gives the terrain guidance: the aircraft's maximum, or what its energy model sustains at the mission speed, whichever is less) and `a` its vertical acceleration limit,
   `G(t) = v0 t + a t^2 / 2` for `t <= (c - v0) / a`, and `G(t) = c t - (c - v0)^2 / (2 a)` after it,
   and a return is unclearable when the climb it needs is more than `G(t)`. This is the exact reachable gain under the two limits, with no fraction in it. `v0` is the pose estimate's vertical velocity.
3. **A margin of its own for what counts as clearing.** `control.avoid.clearance_m` is the clearance a return must be cleared by for it to count as clearable, in place of the planning clearance. The default is the terrain guidance's clearance, so that a scenario that sets none behaves as under ADR 0014. A smaller value makes the rule react when contact is at stake and not whenever the planning margin is; the altitude floor keeps the planning clearance and still raises the command to it. The value is a margin for the uncertainty that the judgment does not otherwise count (the pose estimate's height, a gust, the vehicle's lag), and it is tried at 30 m in the experiment and swept at 15, 30, and 60 m.
4. **All three are options that default to the old behavior.** Runs without them are unchanged to the byte, as for every option so far, and the canonical text writes the keys only when they differ from the defaults, in the same scenario version as `control.avoid`. `feasibility: kinematic` needs the vehicle's vertical acceleration limit, which the scenario builder already gives `avoid`.
5. **A cost measure for staying near the circle.** The metric `orbit_corridor_share`: the share of the (aircraft, record) samples, over aircraft that are alive, whose cross-track distance to their own orbit circle (or pattern path) is within 100 m, computed from the log as `orbit_radial_rmse_m` is, with no change to the binary log. It adds a column to the metrics CSV (36 metric columns). `time_on_station` stays, so that results stay comparable with those of ADR 0014.
6. **The hold time and horizon keep their defaults,** and the experiment includes avoidance as built with the 6 s horizon, which ADR 0014 found to cost a third of the formation error at no loss of safety, so that the effect of these changes can be told from the effect of that one.
7. **No change to the binary log, the estimator rule, or the altitude floor.**

## How it will be judged

The same scenario and steepness levels as ADR 0014, so that new arms can be compared with its results directly, with the levels fixed and committed before the first run, 30 seeds per cell, paired by seed.

- **Arms.** Seven: the forward sensor alone (the baseline of ADR 0014); avoidance as built (`avoid`); with `rejoin` only; with `feasibility: kinematic` only; with `clearance_m: 30` only; with all three; and avoidance as built with a 6 s horizon.
- **Levels.** Hills of 40, 240, 400, and 480 m; the map none and exact; clear air and the fog of 375 m visibility: 4 x 2 x 2 x 7 = 112 cells, 3,360 runs. A sensitivity sweep of the avoidance clearance (15, 30, and 60 m) for the arm with all three, on 400 m hills with no map and with the map 60 m too low: 6 cells, 180 runs.
- **What would count as better, fixed now.** An arm is better than `avoid` if, on the steep hills with no map (400 and 480 m, clear air), its late contacts are not higher than those of `avoid` beyond the paired interval (an increase whose interval is above zero is a loss of safety), and its `orbit_corridor_share` is higher and its radial error lower with intervals that do not include zero; and, with an exact map, if its `time_on_station` is at least 0.90 at every steepness level (the forward sensor alone has 0.94 to 1.00) and its `orbit_corridor_share` is not below that of the forward sensor alone by more than 0.05. An arm that is better on some and worse on others is reported as that, with a table of safety against formation cost for every arm, not a single verdict.
- **Measures.** `late_contacts`, `ground_contacts`, `launch_contacts`, `min_agl_m`, and `first_ground_contact_s` for safety; `orbit_corridor_share`, `time_on_station`, `orbit_radial_rmse_m`, `mean_agl_m`, `collision_episodes`, and `min_separation_m` for cost. `min_agl_m` matters here in particular: a smaller clearance lets the aircraft pass lower than before when it cannot climb, and the experiment must show that this is not a loss of safety.
- **A null result is a result.** If `rejoin` changes nothing, the cause of the wide excursions is something else, and the next step is a closer look at the path, not a third rule.

## Alternatives considered

- **A fixed climb fraction of 0.9 in place of 0.6.** It moves a guess to another guess and keeps the straight-line model of the climb. The kinematic form needs no fraction, and what a margin is for goes into one named number that is swept.
- **A rejoin heading taken from the circle's geometry** (the tangent cut toward the circle, as `orbit_guidance` does). It works for the circle only and would put a second copy of the orbit's direction in the brain; the guidance acceleration is the same information for every pattern, from the one place that computes it.
- **A stronger pull back to the circle after a detour** (a larger radial gain while off the circle after avoiding). It shrinks the excursions at the cost of bank, and the aircraft would turn back toward the hill sooner; the choice of column sets where the detour goes. It can follow if this is not enough.
- **Dropping the hold.** The hold keeps the heading for 3 s after the last threat so that the aircraft clears the ground before the orbit turns it back; removing it would change ADR 0014's rule rather than the return.
- **Planning the whole return** (a path from the aircraft to its slot that avoids the sensed ground). It is a planner, and that question waits until a simple rule shows what is left to gain.
- **Sharing detections between aircraft.** Still the decision after these, and it needs avoidance that works.

## Not in scope

The aircraft's phase on its circle after a detour, launch altitude, the altitude floor's own climb to about 200 m, a sensor with more rays or a wider fan, a quadrotor version, and any claim about the real aircraft.

## Consequences

- The brain uses the guidance acceleration in the avoidance, which is computed before it; a test must show that for a circle on its track the preferred heading is the direction `orbit_guidance` takes, and that for the other patterns it is the direction their own acceleration gives.
- A rejoin preference can turn the aircraft toward ground that the old rule would have turned it away from: the free column nearest the guidance heading is free, not safe in general, because the sensor sees only its fan. The all-blocked case and the trigger are unchanged and keep that guard, and the experiment counts the contacts that this could add.
- A smaller avoidance clearance and the kinematic judgment take away margin: an aircraft that judges a return clearable and then cannot climb as fast (the energy model's thrust at that speed, a gust, the vehicle's lag) reaches it, lower than before. The sweep of the clearance and `min_agl_m` are there to show it.
- Three new keys under `control.avoid` and one new metric column; scenarios without the keys are unchanged, and the canonical text of a scenario that sets them to their defaults is the same as one that sets none.
- Everything about the rule is a modeling choice. The results say what it does in this simulator, not what the real aircraft does.

## Outcome

Recorded on 2026-10-23 after the experiment ran; the numbers are in docs/RESULTS.md. The criteria above were applied as written. Three arms meet every one of them: the clearance of 30 m alone, the three changes together, and the 6 s horizon. The rejoin heading alone and the kinematic judgment alone improve the formation on the steep hills and fail the exact-map criterion, so most of the false alarms were the clearance's. The three together are best: on 400 m hills with no map the share of samples within 100 m of the circle goes from 0.49 to 0.96 and the radial error from 202 m to 34 m, with late contacts 0.37 against 0.30 (the paired difference +0.07 [-0.03, +0.16], so a small loss is not excluded), and with an exact map avoidance costs nothing (time on station 0.92 to 1.00). The clearance sweep shows the clearance is the lever: at 60 m the formation cost returns where the aircraft follow a map that is wrong. What did not improve: time on station on the steep hills (0.14 against 0.30 for the forward sensor alone), because the phase lost in a detour is not recovered, and the residual contacts, which were not examined. The corridor share cannot be compared with the forward sensor alone where that arm loses aircraft, because a crashed aircraft stays in its corridor. Next candidates, not decided here: recovering the phase after a detour, examining the residual contacts, and sharing detections between aircraft.
